Wire-compatible with 0.37.0: a new client streams from an old host and the other way round, so you can update one side at a time. The Windows display driver is unchanged from 0.37.0.

0.38.0 comes days after 0.37.0. It carries fixes we did not want to hold back: a Windows update that left the old display driver running, a stuttering stream on Windows hosts, and a Siri Remote pointer that froze. Picture fit and controller mouse came in with them, and new features belong in a minor release, not a patch.

- **Picture fit: crop or stretch the stream to your screen** on Linux, Windows, Mac, iPhone, iPad, Apple TV and Android. A device joining a display another device sized gets a picture framed for its own screen from Linux hosts with a hardware encoder.
- **Controller mouse.** A dial button turns your controller into the host's mouse, for the launcher or dialog that ignores controllers.
- **Apple TV catches up with the 0.37 app:** tabs, the Library with details, its own presets and dial. The Siri Remote pointer no longer freezes after the first click.
- **Windows: the installer checks the display driver really loaded.** A host that said "driver outdated" after updating to 0.37.0 streams again.
- **Windows hosts stop stuttering on a monitor that keeps waking up**, and a controller keeps its player slot when it reconnects quickly.
- **Library titles show their description, tags and player count** on iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple TV. Windows hosts add Epic, GOG and Xbox app tiles, and Moonlight shows the Desktop and launcher tiles instead of blanks.
- **Game Mode hosts:** the pointer hides when the game hides it, scrolling moves at the right speed, and switching to the desktop no longer freezes the stream for 16 seconds.

## Before you update

- **Windows: Disable monitor devices (PnP) is now on.** The update turns it on. If a monitor fails to come back after a stream, turn it off in the console's display settings; it then stays off.
- **Linux hosts: HDR now wins over 4:4:4.** A client that asks for both gets HDR at 4:2:0, so games offer their HDR toggle. Turn HDR off in the client for 4:4:4 text.

## New

### Picture fit

- **Fit, Crop to fill or Stretch to fill**, in each client's settings and in presets. Fit keeps the whole picture with bars; Crop fills the screen and cuts the edges; Stretch fills it and distorts. Mouse, touch and pen follow the picture you see. A size within a few pixels of 1:1, 2× or 3× snaps to it, so the picture stays sharp. On Android 9 and older, Crop shows the picture squeezed.
- **A joined display framed for the joiner.** A phone joining a 4K desktop on a Linux host with a hardware encoder (AMD, Intel or NVIDIA) gets just the part it shows, at its own size, instead of decoding 4K with bars. Other hosts send the whole picture and the client frames it.
- **Sharper scaling on Linux and Steam Deck clients, and most Windows clients.** A picture at an in-between size draws through sharp scaling filters instead of one soft blur, and whole-number scales stay pixel-exact.

### Controller mouse

- **Add Controller mouse to the quick-action dial** on Linux, Windows, Android and Apple clients. The controller that opened the dial moves the pointer with the left stick, scrolls with the right, clicks with A and X, and B is Escape. The game sees an idle controller. Fire it again to hand the controller back. A dial opened by touch or keyboard switches every connected controller.

### Apple TV

- **Hosts · Library · Settings tab bar.** One swipe up reaches any tab, and Menu inside a tab returns to its top.
- **The Library tab from iPhone and iPad:** the shelf, the host filter and the sections. Holding a title opens its menu; Details opens a full-screen page with the cover, credits, genres and your play record. Customize reorders sections from each row's menu.
- **Settings in categories**, with a sidebar. Codec, 4:4:4, 10-bit SDR, controller routing, Library and Start in now reach the TV, and the focused row's description reads at TV size.
- **Presets made on the TV.** The Editing row creates, renames, duplicates and deletes them, and a changed row shows a dot that a long press resets.
- **A quick-action dial for the TV.** It starts with End stream, Disconnect, Statistics, Guide and Quick access menu, and Settings › Quick Actions edits it.
- **The host page with sections**, as on iPad and Mac. Play/Pause on a host card opens it, and the speed test waits for Start in its own pane.

### Library

- **Descriptions, tags and player counts** from library plugins show on a title's details on iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple TV. A cover a plugin sends inline loads.
- **Epic, GOG and Xbox app tiles on Windows hosts.** A tile whose app is not installed is left out; its games still sync.

### Android

- **Settings → Input → Back opens quick actions.** Turn it off and Back does nothing while the twist, a keyboard or a controller can open the dial.

## Improved

- **iPhone, iPad and Mac:** a stream larger than the screen, such as 2880×2160 on an iPad Pro, downscales through Apple's own scaler, so fine text stays sharp. A host's address, port and Wake-on-LAN address edit in place on its page. Every cover names its host with the host's OS mark.
- **Two-finger scrolling** on Android, iPhone, iPad and touchscreens on Linux and Windows starts from the first movement and moves smoothly, instead of late and in notches.
- **Hyprland is its own compositor choice** on every client, and a Windows host no longer calls itself KDE.
- **PyroWave sessions ask for 4:4:4** on the switch alone, on Linux, Windows and Apple clients whose graphics can't decode HEVC 4:4:4.
- **Android:** with a hardware keyboard attached, the app explains its key service once at launch and asks before turning it on. Keys go only to the streamed computer, and only during a stream.

## Fixed

- **Windows host:** an update could leave the virtual display on the previous driver, and a reboot did not always recover it. The installer now restarts or rebinds the driver, and fails if it still isn't the new one. A controller that dropped and came back within a third of a second got a second controller beside the live one, so games moved it to another player. A host running under an account other than the system one could take a live pad's slot and lose controllers for the rest of the session.
- **Windows hosts on AMD and Intel graphics:** a lost packet in AV1 from an AMD host heals without a full keyframe, and a second loss shortly after the first no longer costs one on AMD or Intel.
- **Linux hosts:** a second device that joins a running session sees the same screen instead of a new display beside it. The first stream after boot has a pointer. On Hyprland, a menu opened by a click from the stream no longer closes at once. On KDE, a game that hides its pointer on a mirrored monitor hides it on the stream, and switching from Game Mode to the Plasma 6 desktop updates its portal. Desktop titles show their icons as library art. The Big Picture tile opens Big Picture when Steam is already running, a launcher tile opens again when you reconnect, and a device set to its own game session launches into it.
- **Gamescope and Game Mode hosts:** the Steam overlay shows on the stream over a game that stops drawing. A game that hides its pointer no longer streams Steam's frozen arrow. Scrolling in Game Mode no longer moves 10 to 16 times too far. Switching from Game Mode to the desktop no longer freezes the stream for 16 seconds, and the next Game Mode session keeps HDR afterwards.
- **Every host:** one out-of-range year or player count from a library plugin no longer fails the whole sync, and a large library with descriptions syncs. A kept display no longer makes the device it was kept for wait at the display limit. On Linux, an update restarts the plugin runner with the host. Moonlight shows the Desktop and launcher tiles with a picture instead of blank.
- **Web console:** a plugin that restarts, as every plugin install does, no longer announces itself as new.
- **Linux, Windows and Steam Deck clients:** the quick-action ring opened invisibly over a host desktop that wasn't changing, so presses could land on End stream unseen. An HEVC stream no longer freezes for good under heavy packet loss. When two machines share a hostname, a saved host card no longer switches to the other machine's address and reads Offline until you restart the app.
- **Linux, Windows and Apple clients:** Select+A opens the dial when the hold-Select gesture is off.
- **iPhone, iPad, Mac and Apple TV:** the speed test chart draws while the test runs, instead of only at the end. The Add Host address field no longer autocorrects or capitalizes. A launch animates out of the tile you pressed.
- **Apple TV:** the Siri Remote pointer froze for the session after the first click, and now keeps moving. It no longer jumps back when your finger lifts, and a swipe aims the quick-action dial by direction. With only the remote, every part of the controller interface is reachable: settings tabs, value steps, host options and the library. A focused launcher tile's logo no longer disappears white on white. The controller legends name the remote's buttons when no controller is paired, and Play/Pause opens Options in the Library. The licenses screen scrolls back to its title, About shows the current app icon, and host options no longer offer a Copy link that does nothing on a TV.
- **Android:** a picture could stay frozen after a still stretch on the host until something else changed. A library restored from cache keeps its developer, release year and genres. A Switch Pro controller's ZL and ZR reach the host.

## Security

- **Hosts and clients take a fixed TLS library.** The one behind pairing, streaming and the web console accepted a TLS 1.3 handshake message at the wrong stage of a connection. Update both sides.
